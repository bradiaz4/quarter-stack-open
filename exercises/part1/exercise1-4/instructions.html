First Aid: How to Stop Severe Bleeding

This guide is for someone who is in an emergency right now. If a person is bleeding heavily, call your local emergency number (EMS) first, or ask someone nearby to call while you help.

What we need

  • A clean cloth, gauze pad, or piece of clean clothing to press on the wound
  • A bandage or strip of cloth about 5 cm wide to hold the pad in place
  • Disposable gloves or a plastic bag for your hands (PPE)
  • A blanket or coat to keep the person warm
  • A phone to call for help

Steps

  1. Make sure the area is safe for you and the injured person, then call for help.
  2. Put on your gloves or cover your hands with a plastic bag.
  3. Press directly on the wound with the cloth, not next to it. Use both hands and lean your weight into it.
  4. Do not stop pressing to check the wound. Lifting the cloth can restart heavy bleeding.
  5. If blood soaks through, add more cloth on top; do not remove the soaked layer.
  6. Have the person lie down and keep them warm. If there is no leg injury, raise their legs.
  7. Once the bleeding slows, bandage the pad firmly in place and stay with the person until help arrives.

If something goes wrong

If the bleeding does not slow after ten minutes of firm pressure, keep pressing and tell the EMS operator. If the person becomes pale, cold, confused, or very sleepy, they may be going into shock, so keep them lying down and warm. If they stop responding and are not breathing normally, begin CPR if you have been trained, and follow the operator's instructions. Never remove an object stuck in the wound, because it may be blocking a blood vessel; press around it instead.

This page gives general first-aid information and does not replace professional medical care or training.
